# How rainy is Seattle, really?

## Introduction

Ask anyone outside the Pacific Northwest about Seattle and the answer comes back quickly: it rains all the time. The claim mixes two different quantities. One is how often it rains, the share of days with any measurable precipitation. The other is how much rain falls over a year. A city can score high on the first and modestly on the second if most of its wet days are light drizzle.

This note separates the two using the daily Seattle record distributed with the Vega datasets, which covers every day from 1 January 2012 to 31 December 2015. Each day carries the precipitation in millimetres, the daily high and low temperature, the mean wind speed and a one-word weather label.

## Frequent, but mostly light

Measurable precipitation fell on 623 of the 1461 days, or 43%. The median wet day brought just 3.8 mm, a light rain. Totals are dominated by a small number of heavy days: the 10% of days with more than 10 mm account for 65% of all the rain in the record. The average annual total is about 1100 mm.

The weather labels tell a similar story from a different angle. Sun is the most common label (49% of days), followed by fog (28%) and rain (18%). Drizzle and snow are rare. The labels are coarse and occasionally disagree with the gauge, so we use them only to describe the texture of a season, never to count rain.

## A strongly seasonal city

The reputation is earned in winter. From October to March, between half and two thirds of days are wet in every month, and November and December each collect more than 150 mm on average. Summer is a different climate: in July only 9% of days are wet and the month averages about 12 mm.

## Conclusion

Seattle is a frequently wet city with a pronounced dry summer, and much of its rain arrives on a few heavy days. Calling it the rainiest city in the country is a statement about how often you need an umbrella, not about how much water falls.
